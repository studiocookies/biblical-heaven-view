Why the seasons happen

  • The tilt stays fixed

    Earth's axis leans 23.4° and keeps pointing toward the North Star all year. In June the north half leans toward the Sun; six months later, on the far side of the orbit, the south half does.

  • Higher sun, longer days

    The hemisphere leaning in gets the Sun higher in the sky and for more hours. Steep light is concentrated on less ground, so each square metre warms more. That is summer.

  • Distance is not the reason

    Earth is closest to the Sun around January 3, in the middle of northern winter. Set the tilt to 0° and every place gets 12 hours of daylight all year: no seasons at all.

Sun positions use the standard low-precision solar formulas (good to about 0.01°) for the year 2026. Daylight includes the usual allowance for sunrise and sunset refraction. Sunrise and sunset are shown in local sun time, not clock time. Coastlines: Natural Earth via world-atlas.
